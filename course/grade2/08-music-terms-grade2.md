# Music Terms (Grade 2)

## What you will learn

- All 24 new terms introduced at Grade 2
- How to group them by category for efficient learning
- The additional dynamic markings (pp, ff, mp, mf) and how they relate to each other

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 2 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to define all **Grade 1 terms** (see Lesson 13) **plus** the Grade 2 terms listed below. All terms may be tested.

## Additional Tempo Terms

| Term | Meaning | Contrast with Grade 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **Largo** | very slow and broad | Slower than Adagio |
| **Lento** | slow | Similar to Adagio |
| **Allegretto** | moderately fast (slightly slower than Allegro) | Between Moderato and Allegro |
| **Vivace** | lively, quick | Faster than Allegro, slightly slower than Presto |

Updated tempo order (slowest to fastest):
**Largo → Lento → Adagio → Andante → Moderato → Allegretto → Allegro → Vivace → Presto**

> **Teaching tip:** The full tempo scale is rarely memorised in order — instead, learn pairs of contrasts. Largo vs Presto (extreme slow vs extreme fast). Allegretto vs Allegro (the suffix *-etto* in Italian means "a little" — so Allegretto = a little Allegro).

## Additional Dynamic Markings

| Symbol | Term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **pp** | pianissimo | very soft |
| **ff** | fortissimo | very loud |
| **mp** | mezzo piano | moderately soft |
| **mf** | mezzo forte | moderately loud |
| **sf / sfz** | sforzando | suddenly forced/accented |

Dynamic scale (softest to loudest): **pp → p → mp → mf → f → ff**

*Mezzo* means "half" or "moderate" in Italian. So *mezzo forte* = "moderately loud."

*Sforzando* (or *sforzato*) indicates a sudden strong accent on a single note or chord — like a sudden punch in the music. It is abbreviated **sf** or **sfz**.

## Expression and Character Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Maestoso** | majestic, stately |
| **Cantabile** | in a singing style |
| **Grazioso** | graceful |
| **Sostenuto** | sustained (notes held for full value or beyond) |
| **Dolce** | sweet, soft |
| **Con brio** | with vigour, with spirit |
| **Risoluto** | resolute, bold |
| **Scherzando** | playfully, jokingly |
| **Tranquillo** | tranquil, calm |

> **Teaching tip:** Act out the character terms physically. "Maestoso" — stand tall and walk regally. "Scherzando" — laugh and skip. "Cantabile" — pretend to sing with big gestures. Connecting terms to physical feelings and actions embeds them far more effectively than rote repetition.

## Performance and Navigation Terms

| Term / Sign | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Da capo** (D.C.) | go back to the beginning |
| **Dal segno** (D.S.) | go back to the sign (𝄋) |
| **Fine** | the end (used with D.C. or D.S.) |
| **D.C. al Fine** | go back to the beginning, play until Fine |
| **D.S. al Fine** | go back to the sign, play until Fine |
| **Coda** | a concluding section (𝄌) |
| **Attacca** | go directly to the next section/movement without pause |
| **Ossia** | an alternative (easier or harder) version of a passage |

## Grouping for Study

Rather than learning all 24 terms in one sitting, group them:

**Group 1 — Speed**: Largo, Lento, Allegretto, Vivace (add to Grade 1 tempo words)
**Group 2 — Loudness**: pp, ff, mp, mf, sfz (add to Grade 1 dynamics)
**Group 3 — Character**: Maestoso, Cantabile, Grazioso, Dolce, Sostenuto, Con brio, Risoluto, Scherzando, Tranquillo
**Group 4 — Navigation**: D.C., D.S., Fine, Coda, Attacca, Ossia

Learn one group per study session, then review all groups together.

## How the examiner tests this

- "What does *Cantabile* mean?" → "In a singing style."
- "What does *sfz* stand for and what does it mean?" → "*Sforzando* — suddenly forced or strongly accented."
- "What is the dynamic level between *p* and *f*?" → "*mp* (mezzo piano) or *mf* (mezzo forte)."
- "What does *D.C. al Fine* instruct the performer to do?" → "Go back to the beginning and play until you reach the word *Fine*."
- "List the following terms in order from slowest to fastest: Vivace, Lento, Allegretto." → "Lento, Allegretto, Vivace."

## Common mistakes

- **Saying *ff* = "fast and loud."** *ff* = fortissimo (very loud only). Fast terms use tempo words, not dynamic letters.
- **Confusing *sostenuto* and *staccato*.** Sostenuto = sustained (long); staccato = short and detached. Opposite concepts.
- **Not knowing the abbreviations.** The exam may show "D.C." or "D.S." — know what each stands for and what it means in context.
- **Confusing *Allegretto* with *Allegro*.** Allegretto is *slower* than Allegro. The *-etto* suffix in Italian is a diminutive (it makes something smaller/less).

## Key points

- Tempo additions: **Largo** (very slow) · **Lento** (slow) · **Allegretto** (moderately fast) · **Vivace** (lively/quick)
- Dynamics: **pp** (very soft) · **mp** (moderately soft) · **mf** (moderately loud) · **ff** (very loud) · **sfz** (suddenly accented)
- Character: **Maestoso** (majestic) · **Cantabile** (singing) · **Grazioso** (graceful) · **Sostenuto** (sustained) · **Dolce** (sweet) · **Con brio** (with spirit) · **Scherzando** (playfully) · **Tranquillo** (calm)
- Navigation: **D.C.** (from beginning) · **D.S.** (from sign) · **Fine** (end) · **Coda** (conclusion) · **Attacca** (no pause between sections)
