# Dotted Notes and Ties

## What you will learn

- How a dot changes a note's duration
- The dotted minim and dotted crotchet (the two tested at Grade 2)
- What a tie is and how it differs from a slur
- How dotted notes relate to compound time

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 2 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to state the value of a **dotted minim** and a **dotted crotchet** in beats, and understand how ties extend note duration.

## The Dot Rule

A dot placed to the right of a note head **adds half the value of that note** to its duration.

In other words: **dotted note = original note + half its value**

| Note | Value | Dotted version | Dotted value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minim | 2 beats | Dotted minim | 2 + 1 = **3 beats** |
| Crotchet | 1 beat | Dotted crotchet | 1 + ½ = **1½ beats** |
| Quaver | ½ beat | Dotted quaver | ½ + ¼ = **¾ beat** |

## The Dotted Minim — 3 Beats

A **dotted minim** is worth 3 crotchet beats. It is equivalent to a minim tied to a crotchet.

```abc
X:1
T:Dotted minim = 3 beats
L:1/4
K:C
c3 d | e3 f |]
```

The dotted minim is very common in 3/4 time — it fills an entire bar perfectly:

```abc
X:1
T:Dotted minim filling a 3/4 bar
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:C
c3 | d3 | e3 |]
```

## The Dotted Crotchet — 1½ Beats

A **dotted crotchet** is worth 1½ crotchet beats. It is almost always followed by a quaver (½ beat) to complete 2 beats together, making a common rhythm pattern.

Dotted crotchet + quaver = 1½ + ½ = **2 beats** ✓

```abc
X:1
T:Dotted crotchet + quaver rhythm
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:C
c3d e3f | g3e c4 |]
```

> **Teaching tip:** This dotted-crotchet-quaver rhythm is one of the most common in music. Clap it: "long-short, long-short." Say: "ta-ta-ti, ta-ta-ti." Students who regularly play piano pieces are almost certainly already playing this rhythm — connecting it to theory makes it click.

## Dotted Notes in 6/8

In 6/8, the **dotted crotchet is the main beat**:
- 1 dotted crotchet = 3 quavers = 1 beat in 6/8
- 2 dotted crotchets fill a complete 6/8 bar

This is why 6/8 is felt in 2 — the two dotted crotchet beats are the natural pulse unit.

```abc
X:1
T:Two dotted crotchet beats filling a 6/8 bar
M:6/8
L:1/8
K:C
c3 d3 | e3 f3 |]
```

## What Is a Tie?

A **tie** is a curved line connecting two notes of the **same pitch**. It means: play the first note and hold it for the combined duration of both notes — do *not* play the second note separately.

```abc
X:1
T:Tie — hold the first note for both durations
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
c2-c d |]
```

In the example above, the C is held for 3 beats (2 + 1), not played again.

Ties are used to:
1. Create note values that no single note symbol can express (e.g., a 3-beat note across a bar line)
2. Hold a note across a bar line when the beat must be preserved in notation

## Tie vs Slur

A **slur** looks identical to a tie — it is also a curved line — but it connects notes of **different pitches**. It means: play smoothly (legato), connecting all the notes under the slur. Do not confuse them:

- **Tie**: same pitch → hold, do not re-attack
- **Slur**: different pitches → play smoothly connected

```abc
X:1
T:Slur — different notes played smoothly
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
(cde f | g2c2 |]
```

## How the examiner tests this

- "How many beats does a dotted minim last?" → "3 crotchet beats."
- "How many beats does a dotted crotchet last?" → "1½ beats."
- "What is the rule for dotted notes?" → "A dot adds half the value of the note."
- "What does a tie do?" → "It joins two notes of the same pitch, holding the first for the combined duration."
- "How is a tie different from a slur?" → "A tie connects notes of the same pitch; a slur connects notes of different pitches and indicates smooth playing."

## Common mistakes

- **Saying a dotted crotchet = 2 beats.** It is 1½ beats. Only when paired with a quaver does the pair total 2 beats.
- **Playing tied notes twice.** The second note in a tie is never re-attacked — the first note just continues longer.
- **Confusing tie and slur when they look the same.** Check the pitches: same = tie, different = slur.
- **Forgetting the dot in 3/4.** A dotted minim fills one 3/4 bar exactly. Without the dot, a minim only lasts 2 of the 3 beats.

## Key points

- **Dot rule**: a dot adds half the note's value (dotted note = note × 1½).
- **Dotted minim** = 3 beats. **Dotted crotchet** = 1½ beats.
- Dotted crotchet + quaver = 2 beats (a very common rhythm pattern).
- In **6/8**, the dotted crotchet is the main beat unit.
- **Tie**: curved line between *same* pitches → hold, do not replay.
- **Slur**: curved line between *different* pitches → play smoothly.
