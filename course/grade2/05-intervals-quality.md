# Intervals — Quality and Number (Grade 2)

## What you will learn

- What interval quality means (major, minor, perfect, diminished)
- How to identify the quality of any interval
- All 12 intervals from unison to octave, with song mnemonics

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 2 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to name all intervals from unison to octave above a given note using the full name: **quality + number** (e.g., "major 3rd", "perfect 4th", "minor 6th"). This applies to intervals built from the notes of the major keys studied at Grades 1 and 2.

## Recap: Interval Numbers

At Grade 1 you counted interval numbers: C to G = 5th, C to E = 3rd. At Grade 2, you add the **quality** — whether the interval is Major (M), Minor (m), Perfect (P) or Diminished (d).

## The Two Quality Systems

Intervals fall into two groups with different quality labels:

**Perfect intervals**: Unison, 4th, 5th, Octave
- These are called "perfect" because they have a pure, stable sound. They are never called Major or Minor.
- They can be diminished (one semitone smaller) or augmented (one semitone larger).

**Major/Minor intervals**: 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th
- In their larger (brighter) form: **Major**
- In their smaller (darker) form: **Minor** (one semitone smaller than major)

## Counting Semitones

After counting the interval number (letter names), count the semitones to determine quality:

| Interval | Semitones | Name |
|---|---|---|
| Unison | 0 | Perfect unison (P1) |
| 2nd | 1 | Minor 2nd (m2) |
| 2nd | 2 | Major 2nd (M2) |
| 3rd | 3 | Minor 3rd (m3) |
| 3rd | 4 | Major 3rd (M3) |
| 4th | 5 | Perfect 4th (P4) |
| 4th | 6 | Diminished 5th / Augmented 4th (d5/A4) |
| 5th | 7 | Perfect 5th (P5) |
| 6th | 8 | Minor 6th (m6) |
| 6th | 9 | Major 6th (M6) |
| 7th | 10 | Minor 7th (m7) |
| 7th | 11 | Major 7th (M7) |
| Octave | 12 | Perfect octave (P8) |

> **Teaching tip:** Work through these on the piano together. Count the keys from C to each interval note. Physically touching each semitone reinforces the count. Then cover the piano and ask: "How many semitones in a major 6th?" This is essential for the aural test as well as the written exam.

## A Method for Any Interval

**Step 1: Count the letter names** (including both) → gives the number.
**Step 2: Count the semitones** (every key, black and white, between the two notes) → gives the quality.
**Step 3: Combine**: "Major 3rd", "Perfect 5th", etc.

**Example:** D to B
- Step 1: D E F G A B = 6 letter names → **6th**
- Step 2: D→Eb(1)→E(2)→F(3)→F#(4)→G(5)→Ab(6)→A(7)→Bb(8)→B(9) = **9 semitones**
- Step 3: 9 semitones + 6th = **Major 6th**

```abc
X:1
T:Major 6th — D up to B
L:1/4
K:C
[DB] |]
```

## The Grade 2 Exam Interval Set

The exam tests all 12 quality names from the table above. The root note may be any note from the keys studied (C, G, D, A, F, Bb, Eb). Always check both letter count and semitone count.


## How the examiner tests this

- "Name this interval." → Show two notes → Answer: "Major 3rd" / "Perfect 5th" / "Minor 7th"
- "Write a major 6th above A."
- "What is the difference between a major 3rd and a minor 3rd?" → "A major 3rd is 4 semitones; a minor 3rd is 3 semitones."
- "Name the interval between F and B." → Count: F G A B = 4th; semitones F→F#→G→Ab→A→Bb→B = 6 semitones → Diminished 5th (augmented 4th). Note: this unusual interval (the tritone) is sometimes tested.

## Common mistakes

- **Using "major" or "minor" for perfect intervals.** You cannot say "major 5th" or "minor 4th" — these intervals are always Perfect (or diminished/augmented).
- **Not counting semitones — guessing instead.** The only reliable method is to count both letter names *and* semitones. Guessing leads to systematic errors.
- **Confusing m3 (3 semitones) and M3 (4 semitones).** Play them back to back. M3 sounds bright; m3 sounds slightly tighter.
- **Forgetting that sharps/flats affect semitone count.** C to E♭ is still a 3rd (C D E = 3 letter names), but 3 semitones → minor 3rd. C to E is also a 3rd, but 4 semitones → major 3rd.

## Key points

- **Perfect intervals** (P1, P4, P5, P8): never called major or minor.
- **Major/Minor intervals** (2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th): major = larger; minor = 1 semitone smaller.
- Method: **count letter names → count semitones → combine quality + number**.
- Major 3rd = 4 semitones; Minor 3rd = 3 semitones. Perfect 5th = 7 semitones. Major 6th = 9 semitones.
- Learn the song mnemonics for aural interval recognition.
