# Cadences in G Major and F Major (Grade 2)

## What you will learn

- How to build cadences in G major and F major
- How the same V → I and IV → I formula applies in any key
- A systematic approach to identifying cadences in any key context

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 2 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to identify **Perfect (V → I)** and **Plagal (IV → I)** cadences in the keys of **C major, G major and F major**, name the chords used, and recognise them aurally.

## Recap: The Two Cadences

- **Perfect cadence**: chord **V → I** (the dominant to tonic — strong conclusion)
- **Plagal cadence**: chord **IV → I** (the subdominant to tonic — softer "Amen" conclusion)

In C major, you already know:
- Perfect: G major → C major (chord V → I)
- Plagal: F major → C major (chord IV → I)

Now apply the same pattern to G major and F major.

## Primary Triads in G Major

In G major (key signature: 1 sharp, F#), the scale is G A B C D E F# G.

The three primary chords are built on degrees I, IV and V of this scale:

| Roman | Degree | Notes | Chord name |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | G (1st) | G B D | G major |
| IV | C (4th) | C E G | C major |
| V | D (5th) | D F# A | D major |

## Complete Summary — All Grade 2 Cadences

| Key | Perfect (V → I) | Plagal (IV → I) |
|---|---|---|
| C major | G major → C major | F major → C major |
| G major | D major → G major | C major → G major |
| F major | C major → F major | Bb major → F major |

## How to Identify a Cadence in Any Key

When given two chords and asked to name the cadence:

1. **Identify the key** (from key signature or context).
2. **Name both chords** (root + major or minor).
3. **Work out their Roman numerals**: which scale degree is each chord built on?
4. **Match the pattern**: V → I = Perfect; IV → I = Plagal.

**Example**: Key of G major. Chords shown: D major → G major.
- In G major, D is the 5th degree (V). G is the 1st degree (I).
- D → G = V → I = **Perfect cadence**.

**Example**: Key of F major. Chords shown: Bb major → F major.
- In F major, Bb is the 4th degree (IV). F is the 1st degree (I).
- Bb → F = IV → I = **Plagal cadence**.

## How the examiner tests this

- Play two chords → "Is this Perfect or Plagal? In what key?"
- "What are the notes of chord V in G major?" → "D, F#, A"
- "Write a Perfect cadence in F major on the treble clef."
- "In G major, which chord is the subdominant (chord IV)?" → "C major"
- "Play the Plagal cadence in G major." → (Practice with the Chord Game)

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting accidentals in chord V.** Chord V in G major = D major = D F# A (the F# must be written even if it's in the key signature). In F major, chord V = C major = C E G (no Bb because C major has no flats).
- **Confusing chord IV and chord V.** In G major, IV = C and V = D. Count up the scale from G: G=1, A=2, B=3, C=4, D=5.
- **Not naming the key.** Always identify the key first — the Roman numerals only make sense relative to a specific key.

## Key points

- **G major primary chords**: I = G major, IV = C major, V = D major (has F#!).
- **F major primary chords**: I = F major, IV = Bb major, V = C major.
- **Perfect (V → I)** in G: D major → G major. In F: C major → F major.
- **Plagal (IV → I)** in G: C major → G major. In F: Bb major → F major.
- To identify a cadence: name the key → name both chords → work out Roman numerals → match pattern.
