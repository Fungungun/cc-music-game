# Minor Chords and New Major Chords (Grade 2)

## What you will learn

- How minor triads are built and how they differ from major triads
- The three Grade 2 minor triads: A minor, D minor and E minor
- The two new major triads: D major and A major

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 2 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to identify (by name and in writing) the following chords in **root position**: **D major, A major, A minor, D minor, E minor**. All Grade 1 chords (C, G, F major) also remain in scope.

## Recap: What Makes a Chord Major or Minor?

A major triad is built on degrees 1, 3, 5 of a major scale. The distance from root to 3rd is **4 semitones** (a major 3rd), and from 3rd to 5th is **3 semitones** (a minor 3rd).

A **minor triad** uses the same root-to-5th span (7 semitones — a perfect 5th), but the 3rd is flattened by one semitone, giving:
- Root to 3rd: **3 semitones** (a minor 3rd)
- 3rd to 5th: **4 semitones** (a major 3rd)

The smaller (minor) 3rd at the bottom gives minor chords their darker, more sorrowful sound.

> **Teaching tip:** Play C major (C E G) then C minor (C Eb G) back to back. Ask: "Can you hear how one sounds bright and one sounds sad?" This is the effect of changing just one note — the 3rd. This is an extremely powerful listening exercise.

## Building a Minor Triad

To build a minor triad from any root note: take the major triad and **lower the 3rd by one semitone**.

Or alternatively: use the **harmonic minor scale** — degrees 1, 3, 5 of the harmonic minor scale produce the minor triad.

## The Three New Minor Triads

### A Minor — A C E

A major triad = A C# E. Lower C# by one semitone to C.
**A minor: A C E**

```abc
X:1
T:A minor triad (root position)
L:2
K:Am
[A,ce] |]
```

A minor is the relative minor of C major — it shares the same key signature (no sharps or flats). The chord A C E uses only white keys, like C major.

### D Minor — D F A

D major triad = D F# A. Lower F# by one semitone to F.
**D minor: D F A**

```abc
X:1
T:D minor triad (root position)
L:2
K:Dm
[DFA] |]
```

D minor is the relative minor of F major.

### E Minor — E G B

E major triad = E G# B. Lower G# by one semitone to G.
**E minor: E G B**

```abc
X:1
T:E minor triad (root position)
L:2
K:Em
[EGB] |]
```

E minor is the relative minor of G major.

## The Two New Major Triads

### D Major — D F# A

Built on degrees 1, 3, 5 of the D major scale (D E F# G A B C# D):
- 1st = D, 3rd = F#, 5th = A

**D major: D F# A**

```abc
X:1
T:D major triad (root position)
L:2
K:D
[D^FA] |]
```

### A Major — A C# E

Built on degrees 1, 3, 5 of the A major scale (A B C# D E F# G# A):
- 1st = A, 3rd = C#, 5th = E

**A major: A C# E**

```abc
X:1
T:A major triad (root position)
L:2
K:A
[A^ce] |]
```

## Major vs Minor — At a Glance

| Chord | Notes | 3rd type |
|---|---|---|
| C major | C E G | Major 3rd (4 semitones) |
| D major | D F# A | Major 3rd |
| A major | A C# E | Major 3rd |
| A minor | A C E | Minor 3rd (3 semitones) |
| D minor | D F A | Minor 3rd |
| E minor | E G B | Minor 3rd |

## Identifying a Chord

When shown a chord in root position:
1. **Name the bottom note** → this is the root (gives the chord name).
2. **Count to the 3rd**: if it is 4 semitones above the root = major; if 3 semitones = minor.
3. **Confirm the 5th**: should be 7 semitones above the root (perfect 5th) in both major and minor.

## How the examiner tests this

- "Name this chord." → Show A minor root position → "A minor triad."
- "Write the D minor chord in root position on the treble clef."
- "What is the difference between D major and D minor?" → "D major has F#; D minor has F natural. The 3rd is lowered by one semitone in D minor."
- "Name the notes in the E minor triad." → "E, G, B."
- "Build an A major triad on the piano." → "A, C#, E."

## Common mistakes

- **Writing F in D major instead of F#.** D major has F# (from the D major scale). Forgetting the sharp gives you D minor instead.
- **Writing C in A major instead of C#.** A major has C# (from the A major scale). This is the 3rd.
- **Confusing the relative pairs.** A minor and A major are *not* relative keys — they share the same root, not the same key signature. Relative minor = different root, same key sig.
- **Forgetting which chord is root position.** Root position means the *named note* is the *lowest* note. If you are asked for A minor in root position, A must be at the bottom.

## Key points

- **Minor triad**: lower the 3rd of the major triad by one semitone. Root→3rd = 3 semitones (minor 3rd); 3rd→5th = 4 semitones (major 3rd).
- **A minor**: A C E. **D minor**: D F A. **E minor**: E G B.
- **D major**: D F# A. **A major**: A C# E.
- Root position: the root (named note) is always the lowest note.
- Major sounds bright (larger interval at bottom); minor sounds darker (smaller interval at bottom).
