# Compound Time — 6/8

## What you will learn

- The difference between simple and compound time
- How 6/8 works and how to count it
- Why 6/8 is called "compound duple" time
- How to distinguish 6/8 from 3/4 by listening

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 2 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to identify **6/8** as compound duple time, explain the difference between simple and compound time, and state how many beats are in a bar of 6/8 and what type of note receives one beat.

## Simple Time — a Quick Recap

In **simple time** (2/4, 3/4, 4/4), each beat divides naturally into **2 equal parts**:
- 1 crotchet beat splits into 2 quavers

The bottom number tells you the beat note:
- 4 = crotchet beat
- 2 = minim beat

## What Is Compound Time?

In **compound time**, each beat divides naturally into **3 equal parts** rather than 2. This creates a flowing, lilting character.

The most common compound time signature is **6/8**:
- 6 quavers per bar
- They group into 2 main beats (each beat = 3 quavers = 1 dotted crotchet)
- So 6/8 is **compound duple** (2 compound beats)

The key insight: **6/8 is conducted and felt in 2**, not in 6.

## Understanding 6/8

```abc
X:1
T:6/8 — felt as 2 dotted crotchet beats
M:6/8
L:1/8
K:C
(3cde (3fga | (3gec (3cde | d6 |]
```

Each group of 3 quavers forms one compound beat. In performance, you feel:
**ONE-and-a, TWO-and-a | ONE-and-a, TWO-and-a**

At a slow tempo, you can hear all 6 quavers. At a faster tempo, you feel only 2 big beats per bar — this is the natural feel of compound duple time.

## Simple vs Compound: The Key Difference

| | Simple time | Compound time |
|---|---|---|
| Beat divides into | 2 equal parts | 3 equal parts |
| Basic beat note | Crotchet (or minim/quaver) | Dotted crotchet (or dotted minim/dotted quaver) |
| Example | 3/4 (3 crotchet beats) | 6/8 (2 dotted crotchet beats) |
| Time signature bottom | 4, 2, 8 | 8, 4, 16 |

> **Teaching tip:** Clap 3/4 as "ONE-two-three, ONE-two-three" (waltz feel). Then clap 6/8 as "ONE-and-a-TWO-and-a" (rocking feel). Play the same rhythm and have your student decide: is it 3/4 or 6/8? The easiest test is: do you feel 3 equal beats or 2 beats that each split into 3?

## 3/4 vs 6/8 — How to Tell Them Apart

Both 3/4 and 6/8 have 6 quaver pulses per bar, but they *feel* completely different:

- **3/4**: three groups of 2 — **[ONE-two] [two-and] [three-and]** (waltz)
- **6/8**: two groups of 3 — **[ONE-and-a] [TWO-and-a]** (compound swing)

The crucial test: **Where is the accent?** In 3/4, the accent falls every 2 quavers (on beats 1, 2 and 3). In 6/8, the accent falls every 3 quavers (on beats 1 and 4).

Famous examples:
- **3/4**: "Morning" from Peer Gynt (Grieg), "Für Elise" (waltz section)
- **6/8**: "Humpty Dumpty" nursery rhyme, "Row Row Row Your Boat", "Compound Time" feel in Irish jigs

## Naming Compound Time Signatures

| Beats per bar | Compound name | Common signature |
|---|---|---|
| 2 compound beats | Compound duple | 6/8 |
| 3 compound beats | Compound triple | 9/8 (Grade 3) |
| 4 compound beats | Compound quadruple | 12/8 |

At Grade 2, you only need **6/8** (compound duple). 9/8 is Grade 3.

## How the examiner tests this

- "What time signature is this piece in?" → The examiner plays something in 6/8 → "6/8"
- "What does the 6/8 time signature mean?" → "Six quaver beats per bar, grouped into two compound beats of three quavers each."
- "What type of time is 6/8?" → "Compound duple time."
- "What is the difference between simple and compound time?" → "In simple time, beats divide into 2; in compound time, beats divide into 3."
- "How many dotted crotchet beats are in a bar of 6/8?" → "Two."

## Common mistakes

- **Saying 6/8 has 6 beats.** At performance tempo, 6/8 is conducted and felt in 2, not 6. The 6 refers to the number of quavers, but the pulse is 2 dotted crotchet beats.
- **Confusing the bottom number.** In compound time, the bottom number refers to the *subdivision* note (quaver = 8), not the actual beat note (dotted crotchet).
- **Not recognising 6/8 by ear.** Practice clapping 3/4 vs 6/8 until the feel is instinctive. The exam includes aural identification.

## Key points

- **Simple time**: each beat divides into **2**. **Compound time**: each beat divides into **3**.
- **6/8**: 6 quavers per bar; felt as **2 dotted crotchet beats** — compound duple.
- Count 6/8 as: **ONE-and-a, TWO-and-a**.
- 3/4 and 6/8 both have 6 quaver pulses, but 3/4 is **3 groups of 2** while 6/8 is **2 groups of 3**.
- The exam may ask whether a piece is in simple or compound time, and ask you to name the time signature.
