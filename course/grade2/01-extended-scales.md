# Extended Scales (Grade 2)

## What you will learn

- Four new major scales: D, A, Bb and Eb
- Two new harmonic minor scales: G and C
- How the circle of fifths helps you predict which accidentals you'll need

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 2 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to name the notes of — or write on the staff — the following scales (ascending and descending): **D major, A major, Bb major, Eb major**; **G harmonic minor, C harmonic minor**. All Grade 1 scales also remain in scope.

## Recap: The Major Scale Formula

Every major scale follows: **T T S T T T S** (Tone Tone Semitone Tone Tone Tone Semitone).

Apply this formula from any starting note to find which accidentals are needed.

## The New Major Scales

### D Major — 2 Sharps (F#, C#)

Starting on D: D→E (T), E→F# (T), F#→G (S), G→A (T), A→B (T), B→C# (T), C#→D (S).

**Notes:** D E F# G A B C# D

The formula needs a tone between E and the 3rd note, so F must be raised to F#. It also needs a tone between B and the 7th note, so C must be raised to C#.

```abc
X:1
T:D major scale
L:1/4
K:D
D E ^F G A B ^c d |]
```

### A Major — 3 Sharps (F#, C#, G#)

Starting on A: A→B (T), B→C# (T), C#→D (S), D→E (T), E→F# (T), F#→G# (T), G#→A (S).

**Notes:** A B C# D E F# G# A

```abc
X:1
T:A major scale
L:1/4
K:A
A B ^c d e ^f ^g a |]
```

### Bb Major — 2 Flats (Bb, Eb)

Starting on Bb: Bb→C (T), C→D (T), D→Eb (S), Eb→F (T), F→G (T), G→A (T), A→Bb (S).

**Notes:** Bb C D Eb F G A Bb

```abc
X:1
T:Bb major scale
L:1/4
K:Bb
_B c d _e f g a _b |]
```

### Eb Major — 3 Flats (Bb, Eb, Ab)

Starting on Eb: Eb→F (T), F→G (T), G→Ab (S), Ab→Bb (T), Bb→C (T), C→D (T), D→Eb (S).

**Notes:** Eb F G Ab Bb C D Eb

```abc
X:1
T:Eb major scale
L:1/4
K:Eb
_E F G _A _B c d _e |]
```

## The New Harmonic Minor Scales

### G Harmonic Minor

G harmonic minor is the relative minor of Bb major (same key signature: Bb). The natural minor would be G A Bb C D Eb F G. Raise the 7th note (F→F#) to get the harmonic minor.

**Notes:** G A Bb C D Eb F# G

```abc
X:1
T:G harmonic minor scale
L:1/4
K:Gm
G A _B c d _e ^f g |]
```

### C Harmonic Minor

C harmonic minor is the relative minor of Eb major (same key signature: Bb, Eb, Ab). Natural minor: C D Eb F G Ab Bb C. Raise the 7th note (Bb→B♮) to get the harmonic minor.

**Notes:** C D Eb F G Ab B C

```abc
X:1
T:C harmonic minor scale
L:1/4
K:Cm
C D _E F G _A =B c |]
```

Note: the natural sign (=B) cancels the key signature's Bb to give B natural, which is the raised 7th.

> **Teaching tip:** Point out that in C harmonic minor, there is an unusual "gap" between Ab and B — this is an augmented 2nd (3 semitones). This distinctive sound is what makes harmonic minor so recognisable. Play Ab then B and ask your student: "Does that sound like it is from a major scale?"

## The Circle of Fifths — A Pattern to Notice

As you add more sharps, each new key is a **5th above** the previous one:
C → G (1#) → D (2#) → A (3#) ...

As you add more flats, each new key is a **5th below** (or a 4th above):
C → F (1b) → Bb (2b) → Eb (3b) ...

This pattern is the **circle of fifths**. Knowing it means you never have to memorise sharps and flats randomly — you can work them out systematically.

## How the examiner tests this

- "Name the notes of the D major scale."
- "Write the Bb major scale on the treble clef."
- "What are the accidentals in A major?"
- "Write the G harmonic minor scale." (Remember: Bb key signature + raised 7th F#)
- "What note is the raised 7th in C harmonic minor?" → "B natural (raised from Bb)"

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the raised 7th in harmonic minor.** The key signature gives the natural minor — you must add the raise manually.
- **Writing G natural in C harmonic minor.** The 7th of C minor is B (not G). Count 7 notes from C: C D E F G A B.
- **Using wrong number of accidentals.** D major has 2 sharps (F#, C#) — not just one (from G major). Each new sharp key adds one more sharp.

## Key points

- **D major**: 2 sharps (F#, C#). **A major**: 3 sharps (F#, C#, G#).
- **Bb major**: 2 flats (Bb, Eb). **Eb major**: 3 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab).
- **G harmonic minor**: G A Bb C D Eb **F#** G — raised 7th is F#.
- **C harmonic minor**: C D Eb F G Ab **B** C — raised 7th is B natural (cancels the Bb).
- The circle of fifths: each sharp key is a 5th above the previous; each flat key is a 5th below.
