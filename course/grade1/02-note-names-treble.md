# Note Names on the Treble Clef

## What you will learn

- The names of every line and space on the treble clef staff
- Memory tricks to recall them quickly
- How to read ledger lines above and below the staff

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 1 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to name any note on the treble clef staff, including notes on the first ledger line above and below the staff (D5 above, B3 below).

## The Seven Note Names

Western music uses seven note names, cycling in order: **A B C D E F G**, then back to A. On the piano, these are the white keys. On the treble clef staff, the notes follow the same alphabetical order upwards — lines and spaces alternate as you climb higher.

## The Lines of the Treble Clef

The five lines, from bottom to top, are: **E G B D F**

```abc
X:1
T:Treble clef lines — E G B D F
L:1/4
K:C
e g b d' f' |]
```

A popular way to remember them: **E**very **G**ood **B**oy **D**eserves **F**ruit.

(You may also see "Every Good Boy Does Fine" or "Edward Gets Big Doing Football" — any phrase that starts with E G B D F works perfectly.)

> **Teaching tip:** Have your student invent their own sentence using E G B D F. A personal, silly sentence is often remembered far better than a textbook one. "Elephants Go By During Floods" — whatever sticks!

## The Spaces of the Treble Clef

The four spaces, from bottom to top, spell a word: **F A C E**

```abc
X:1
T:Treble clef spaces — F A C E
L:1/4
K:C
f a c' e' |]
```

The spaces spell FACE — easy to remember because they spell a real word. The bottom space is F4, then A4, C5, E5.

## Reading Notes in Order

Line and space notes alternate as you go up the staff. Starting from the bottom:

| Position | Note |
|---|---|
| Line 1 (bottom) | E |
| Space 1 | F |
| Line 2 | G |
| Space 2 | A |
| Line 3 (middle) | B |
| Space 3 | C |
| Line 4 | D |
| Space 4 | E |
| Line 5 (top) | F |

```abc
X:1
T:All notes — E through F (treble clef)
L:1/4
K:C
e f g a b c' d' e' f' |]
```

## Ledger Lines Above and Below

When notes go above or below the five-line staff, short extra lines called **ledger lines** are used. Middle C (C4) sits on the first ledger line below the treble staff. Notes above the top line (F5) continue on ledger lines above.

```abc
X:1
T:Middle C — one ledger line below the treble staff
L:1
K:C
c |]
```

```abc
X:1
T:High notes — G5 and A5 (above the staff)
L:1/4
K:C
g' a' |]
```

When reading ledger lines, simply continue the alphabet in the same alternating pattern. Count carefully from the last line of the staff.

## How the examiner tests this

The examiner will show you a treble clef staff with arrows or dots pointing to specific notes and ask you to name them. They may:
- Ask for the letter name only ("What note is this?")
- Ask you to write a specific note on a blank treble staff ("Write a B on the staff")
- Show a short melody and ask you to name each note in order

## Common mistakes

- **Starting from the top instead of the bottom.** Always count lines from the bottom (line 1) upwards. Starting from the top gives you the wrong answer.
- **Skipping a line or space when counting.** If in doubt, count every position — line, space, line, space — starting from E on line 1.
- **Confusing the B (middle line) with the A just below it.** B sits *on* line 3; A sits *in* the space just below line 3.

## Key points

- Treble clef lines (bottom to top): **E G B D F** ("Every Good Boy Deserves Fruit")
- Treble clef spaces (bottom to top): **F A C E** (spells "FACE")
- Lines and spaces alternate; note names always go in alphabetical order upwards.
- Middle C sits one ledger line below the treble staff.
- Count from the bottom when identifying any note.
