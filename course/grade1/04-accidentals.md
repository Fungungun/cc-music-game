# Accidentals — Sharps, Flats and Naturals

## What you will learn

- What sharps, flats and naturals do to a note
- How to recognise these symbols on the staff
- The rule for how long an accidental lasts

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 1 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to identify the signs for sharp (♯), flat (♭) and natural (♮), state what each does to a note (raises/lowers by one semitone; cancels), and explain how long an accidental applies.

## What Is an Accidental?

Music mostly uses the seven natural notes: A B C D E F G. However, we also use the black keys of the piano — and to write those, we use symbols called **accidentals**. An accidental is written directly before a note on the staff.

## The Sharp ( ♯ )

A **sharp** raises a note by one **semitone** — that is, one step to the right on the piano keyboard (the very next key, whether white or black).

```abc
X:1
T:F sharp — one semitone higher than F
L:1/4
K:C
f ^f |]
```

In ABC notation, `^f` means F sharp. On the piano, F# is the black key immediately to the right of F.

- C# is the black key immediately to the right of C
- F# is the black key immediately to the right of F
- G# is the black key immediately to the right of G

## The Flat ( ♭ )

A **flat** lowers a note by one semitone — one step to the left on the piano keyboard.

```abc
X:1
T:B flat — one semitone lower than B
L:1/4
K:C
b _b |]
```

In ABC notation, `_b` means B flat. On the piano, Bb is the black key immediately to the left of B.

- Bb is the black key immediately to the left of B
- Eb is the black key immediately to the left of E
- Ab is the black key immediately to the left of A

## The Natural ( ♮ )

A **natural** cancels a previous sharp or flat and returns the note to its original pitch.

```abc
X:1
T:B flat cancelled by a natural
L:1/4
K:C
_b =b |]
```

In a piece that has a key signature (for example, G major has F# throughout), a natural sign on F tells the player to play F natural instead of F# in that bar.

## The Rule: Accidentals Last for One Bar

An accidental written in front of a note applies to **every note of the same pitch in the same bar**, not just the one it is written next to. At the next bar line, the accidental automatically cancels — notes return to their original pitch unless a new accidental is written.

> **Teaching tip:** Find a sharp or flat in your student's current piano piece. Show them the bar line and ask: "Does the sharp still apply after this bar line?" This is a common trap in sight-reading as well as in the theory exam.

## Semitones and Tones

An important pair of terms used throughout theory:

- **Semitone**: the smallest step in Western music — one key to the right or left on the piano (e.g., C to C#, E to F, B to C).
- **Tone**: two semitones (e.g., C to D, F to G, A to B).

Note that some neighbouring white keys are only a semitone apart — E to F and B to C have no black key between them.

```abc
X:1
T:E and F are a semitone apart — no black key between them
L:1/4
K:C
e f |]
```

## How the examiner tests this

The examiner may:
- Show a note with a sharp or flat and ask: "What is the name of this note?"
- Ask: "What does a sharp/flat/natural sign do to a note?"
- Show a bar with an accidental and ask whether a later note in the same bar is affected
- Ask you to add an accidental to make a named note (e.g., "Add a flat to make this note B flat")

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting that sharps raise and flats lower by one semitone only.** A sharp on C gives you C#, not D.
- **Thinking E# and B# are impossible.** E# = F (same key), B# = C. These exist but are unusual and not tested at Grade 1.
- **Forgetting the bar rule.** An accidental *does* carry through to the end of the bar, and *does not* carry past the bar line.
- **Placing the accidental after the note.** The accidental always goes *before* (to the left of) the note head.

## Key points

- **Sharp (♯)**: raises a note by one semitone (one key to the right on the piano).
- **Flat (♭)**: lowers a note by one semitone (one key to the left on the piano).
- **Natural (♮)**: cancels a previous sharp or flat.
- A **semitone** is the smallest step; a **tone** is two semitones.
- E to F and B to C are semitones — there is no black key between them.
- An accidental applies for the rest of the bar, then cancels at the bar line.
