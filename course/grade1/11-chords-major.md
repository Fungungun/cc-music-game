# Major Triads

## What you will learn

- What a chord is and what a triad is
- How to build a major triad from a major scale
- The three Grade 1 major triads: C, G and F

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 1 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to identify C major, G major and F major triads in root position — by name, by notes, and by recognition on the staff. They must be able to build any of these chords on the piano keyboard or on a blank staff.

## What Is a Chord?

A **chord** is three or more notes sounded at the same time. Chords provide the harmonic foundation (the "backing") for melodies. In piano music, chords are often played with the left hand while the right hand plays the melody.

A **triad** is the simplest type of chord — it has exactly three notes.

## Building a Major Triad

The easiest way to build a major triad is to use the 1st, 3rd and 5th notes of the major scale. These three degrees are always present in the triad.

| Scale degree | Role |
|---|---|
| 1st (root) | Bottom note — gives the chord its name |
| 3rd | Middle note — determines major or minor character |
| 5th | Top note |

```abc
X:1
T:Degrees 1, 3, 5 of C major
L:1/4
K:C
c e g |]
```

## C Major Triad

Take the C major scale: C D E F G A B C

- 1st = C (root)
- 3rd = E
- 5th = G

**C major chord: C E G**

```abc
X:1
T:C major triad (root position)
L:2
K:C
[ceg] |]
```

On the piano, this is C + E + G played together — all white keys.

## G Major Triad

Take the G major scale: G A B C D E F# G

- 1st = G
- 3rd = B
- 5th = D

**G major chord: G B D**

```abc
X:1
T:G major triad (root position)
L:2
K:C
[GBd] |]
```

Note that D is below middle C here (D3), with G and B above (G3 and B3). When written on treble clef with the root in the third space below the staff, the chord still reads G–B–D from bottom to top.

## F Major Triad

Take the F major scale: F G A Bb C D E F

- 1st = F
- 3rd = A
- 5th = C

**F major chord: F A C**

```abc
X:1
T:F major triad (root position)
L:2
K:C
[FAc] |]
```

## Root Position

When the root (the named note) is at the bottom of the chord, we say the chord is in **root position**. At Grade 1, all chords are in root position. (Inversions — where the 3rd or 5th is at the bottom — are tested at Grade 3.)

> **Teaching tip:** Play each triad on the piano. Ask your student to identify which is C, G or F major. Then ask them to build each one from scratch using the scale formula. Finally, show how these same chords appear in their current piano piece — they will almost certainly be playing all three.

## The Sound of a Major Chord

Major chords have a bright, positive, confident sound. This is because of the specific semitone distances between the notes:

- Root to 3rd: **4 semitones** (a major 3rd)
- 3rd to 5th: **3 semitones** (a minor 3rd)

Total from root to 5th: 7 semitones (a perfect 5th)

Minor chords (Grade 2) have the same total (7 semitones, root to 5th) but with a different split: 3 semitones then 4 semitones — which gives them their darker character.

## Primary Chords

In any key, the three most important chords are built on the 1st, 4th and 5th degrees of the scale. These are called the **primary triads** and are labelled with Roman numerals:

- **I** (one) — the tonic chord (built on the 1st note)
- **IV** (four) — the subdominant chord (built on the 4th note)
- **V** (five) — the dominant chord (built on the 5th note)

In C major:
- Chord I = C major (C E G)
- Chord IV = F major (F A C)
- Chord V = G major (G B D)

These three chords are used for cadences (the next lesson).

## How the examiner tests this

- "Name this chord." → Show a root-position major triad on the staff → Answer: "C major triad" or "G major chord"
- "Build the F major chord on the treble clef."
- "Which degree of the scale is the lowest note of a root-position chord?" → Answer: "The 1st degree (the root)"
- "Name the notes in the G major triad." → Answer: "G, B, D"

## Common mistakes

- **Using the wrong 3rd.** In G major, the 3rd note of the scale is B — but beginners sometimes write C instead of counting carefully from G.
- **Forgetting to use scale notes.** The notes of the chord must come from the scale. G major triad is G B D, not G A D.
- **Adding extra notes.** A triad has exactly three notes. Adding a 4th note creates a different type of chord.

## Key points

- A **triad** is a chord of three notes built on the 1st, 3rd and 5th degrees of a scale.
- **C major triad**: C E G. **G major triad**: G B D. **F major triad**: F A C.
- **Root position**: the root (named note) is the lowest note of the chord.
- Major chords sound bright: root → 3rd = 4 semitones; 3rd → 5th = 3 semitones.
- The primary chords in any key are built on degrees I, IV and V.
