# Music Terms and Signs (Grade 1)

## What you will learn

- Why musical terms are written in Italian
- The 17 Grade 1 music terms and their meanings
- How to group terms by category for easier learning

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 1 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to define the meaning of Italian terms and signs as listed for Grade 1, and identify signs used in a musical score (e.g., sharp, flat, natural, pause, repeat signs).

## Why Italian?

From the 16th to 18th centuries, Italy was the centre of Western musical culture. Italian composers like Vivaldi, Corelli and Monteverdi published music across Europe, and they used Italian words to describe how their music should be performed. The convention spread and stuck — even today, when a composer from any country writes a tempo or dynamic marking, they usually write it in Italian.

## Tempo Terms — How Fast

Tempo means speed. The following terms are in order from slowest to fastest:

| Term | Meaning | Approximate feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Adagio** | slowly | very slow, expressive |
| **Andante** | at a walking pace | gently moving |
| **Moderato** | moderately | medium speed |
| **Allegro** | fast, lively | brisk and energetic |
| **Presto** | very fast | as fast as possible |

> **Teaching tip:** Play the same short melody at each of these tempos. Ask: "Which one sounds like a lullaby? Which sounds like a race?" Connecting tempo words to real-life experiences makes them stick. *Andante* literally means "going" in Italian (from *andare* = to walk).

## Tempo Change Terms — Changing Speed

| Term | Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Accelerando** | accel. | gradually getting faster |
| **Rallentando** | rall. | gradually getting slower |
| **Ritardando** | rit. | gradually getting slower (same as rall.) |
| **Ritenuto** | riten. | immediately (suddenly) slower |
| **A tempo** | a tempo | return to the original tempo |

*Rall.* and *rit.* both mean gradually slower and are often used interchangeably, though technically *ritardando* suggests a more hesitant slowing while *rallentando* suggests a more natural broadening. For exam purposes, they have the same meaning.

*Ritenuto* is different — it means *immediately* slower, not gradually. Think of it as applying the brakes suddenly.

## Dynamic Terms — How Loud

Dynamics describe volume levels or changes in volume.

| Term / Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **f** (forte) | loud |
| **p** (piano) | soft / quiet |
| **Crescendo** (cresc. or ——<) | gradually getting louder |
| **Decrescendo / Diminuendo** (decresc. / dim. or >——) | gradually getting softer |

*Forte* and *piano* are abbreviated as single letters — **f** and **p** — printed in italics on the score. The "hairpin" symbols (< for crescendo, > for decrescendo) are the graphical equivalents written directly into the score.

## Articulation Terms — How Notes Are Played

| Term | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Legato** | slur (curved line) | smoothly, notes connected |
| **Staccato** | dot above/below note | short and detached |

*Legato* comes from the Italian *legare* = to bind or tie. When you play legato, each note flows into the next without any gap.

*Staccato* comes from *staccare* = to detach. When you play staccato, each note is cut short, leaving a small silence between notes.

## Signs You Must Recognise

Beyond written terms, the AMEB exam may ask you to identify symbols in a score:

| Sign | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ♯ | Sharp | Raise by a semitone |
| ♭ | Flat | Lower by a semitone |
| ♮ | Natural | Cancel a sharp or flat |
| 𝄋 | Pause / Fermata | Hold the note longer than written |
| 𝄆…𝄇 | Repeat signs | Repeat the section between the signs |
| D.C. | Da Capo | Go back to the beginning |
| D.S. | Dal Segno | Go back to the sign (%) |
| Fine | Fine | The end (when used with D.C. or D.S.) |

## How the examiner tests this

- "What does *Allegro* mean?" → "Fast and lively."
- "What is the difference between *rit.* and *riten.*?" → "Both mean slower, but *rit.* is gradual while *riten.* is immediate."
- "What does this symbol mean?" (pointing to a crescendo hairpin) → "Getting gradually louder."
- "What does *a tempo* tell the performer to do?" → "Return to the original speed."
- "Name three Italian terms that describe tempo." → Any three from the tempo list.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing *forte* (f) with "fast."** Forte means *loud*, not fast. Fast = Allegro/Presto.
- **Confusing *piano* (p, the dynamic) with "piano" (the instrument).** Context makes this clear, but the exam will use *p* for the dynamic.
- **Saying *rit.* and *riten.* are the same.** They are similar but *riten.* is immediate, not gradual.
- **Not knowing abbreviations.** The exam may show "accel." without the full word — know all abbreviations.

## Key points

- Tempo (fastest to slowest): **Presto → Allegro → Moderato → Andante → Adagio**
- Tempo changes: **accel.** = gradually faster; **rall./rit.** = gradually slower; **riten.** = immediately slower; **a tempo** = back to original speed.
- Dynamics: **f** = loud; **p** = soft; **cresc.** = louder; **dim./decresc.** = softer.
- Articulation: **legato** = smooth; **staccato** = detached.
- Know the sign names: sharp, flat, natural, pause, repeat signs, D.C., D.S., Fine.
