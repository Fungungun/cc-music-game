# Note Names on the Bass Clef

## What you will learn

- The names of every line and space on the bass clef staff
- Memory tricks to recall them
- How middle C connects the two staves

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 1 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to name any note on the bass clef staff, including notes on the first ledger line above and below the staff. They must also be able to locate and identify middle C on both the treble and bass clef.

## The Bass Clef

The bass clef covers lower pitches — roughly the notes played by the left hand on the piano. Its symbol resembles a backwards C with a dot, and two dots on either side of the fourth line (which marks F). The note names still go up the staff in alphabetical order, but they start from different positions than the treble clef.

## The Lines of the Bass Clef

The five lines, from bottom to top, are: **G B D F A**

```abc
X:1
T:Bass clef lines — G B D F A
L:1/4
K:C clef=bass
G, B, D F A |]
```

Memory sentence: **G**ood **B**oys **D**eserve **F**udge **A**lways.

(Alternatives: "Great Big Dogs Fight Animals", "Go Buy Dad Fine Apples" — again, invent one that suits your student.)

> **Teaching tip:** Notice that the bass clef lines are *different* from the treble clef lines. A note sitting on line 2 is G in treble clef but B in bass clef. Always check which clef you are in before reading a note.

## The Spaces of the Bass Clef

The four spaces, from bottom to top, are: **A C E G**

```abc
X:1
T:Bass clef spaces — A C E G
L:1/4
K:C clef=bass
A, C E G |]
```

Memory sentence: **A**ll **C**ows **E**at **G**rass.

(Or: "All Cars Eat Gas".)

## Reading Notes in Order

| Position | Note |
|---|---|
| Line 1 (bottom) | G |
| Space 1 | A |
| Line 2 | B |
| Space 2 | C |
| Line 3 (middle) | D |
| Space 3 | E |
| Line 4 | F |
| Space 4 | G |
| Line 5 (top) | A |

```abc
X:1
T:All notes — G through A (bass clef)
L:1/4
K:C clef=bass
G, A, B, C D E F G A |]
```

## Middle C: Where the Two Staves Meet

**Middle C** is the same key on the piano regardless of which clef it is written in. In the bass clef, middle C appears one ledger line *above* the top of the staff. In the treble clef, it appears one ledger line *below* the bottom of the staff.

```abc
X:1
T:Middle C on bass clef (one ledger line above)
L:1
K:C clef=bass
c |]
```

Think of the two staves as a mirror, with middle C acting as the meeting point in the middle. Piano music almost always shows middle C in whichever clef makes it easier to read — whichever is closest to the note.

## Comparing the Two Clefs Side by Side

| Treble clef lines | Bass clef lines |
|---|---|
| E G B D F | G B D F A |
| (line 5 = F5) | (line 5 = A3) |

The bass clef sounds lower overall. The highest note comfortably on the bass staff (A3) is lower in pitch than the lowest comfortable note on the treble staff (E4).

## How the examiner tests this

- The examiner shows a note on the bass clef and asks: "What note is this?"
- They may show notes in both treble and bass clef in the same piece and ask you to name each one.
- They may ask: "Where is middle C on the bass clef?"

## Common mistakes

- **Using treble clef note names for bass clef notes.** The note on line 2 is G in treble clef, but B in bass clef. The mnemonic for each clef is different.
- **Forgetting that the bass clef is lower in pitch.** Students sometimes mentally "translate" bass clef notes as if they were treble, making the pitch sound wrong.
- **Middle C confusion.** Middle C on bass clef is *above* the staff; on treble clef it is *below*. Both are the same note.

## Key points

- Bass clef lines (bottom to top): **G B D F A** ("Good Boys Deserve Fudge Always")
- Bass clef spaces (bottom to top): **A C E G** ("All Cows Eat Grass")
- Bass clef notes are lower in pitch than treble clef notes.
- Middle C sits one ledger line above the bass staff and one ledger line below the treble staff — it is the same note.
- Always check the clef symbol before naming a note.
