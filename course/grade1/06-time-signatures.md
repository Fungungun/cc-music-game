# Time Signatures

## What you will learn

- What a time signature is and how to read it
- The difference between 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4
- Bar lines, bars and how beats are grouped
- Strong and weak beats

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 1 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to identify the time signatures 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4; explain what the top and bottom numbers mean; and identify whether a piece is in duple (2/4), triple (3/4) or quadruple (4/4) time — both by reading and by listening.

## What Is a Time Signature?

A **time signature** appears at the very beginning of a piece, immediately after the key signature. It looks like a fraction, with two numbers stacked on top of each other:

- The **top number** tells you how many beats are in each bar.
- The **bottom number** tells you what kind of note gets one beat (4 = crotchet, 2 = minim, 8 = quaver).

For all Grade 1 time signatures, the bottom number is **4**, meaning the crotchet gets one beat.

```abc
X:1
T:4/4 time — four crotchet beats per bar
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
c d e f | g f e d | c4 |]
```

## Bar Lines and Bars

Music is divided into equal sections of beats called **bars** (or **measures**). A thin vertical line called a **bar line** separates one bar from the next. A double bar line (thin-thick) marks the end of a piece. Every bar must contain exactly the number of beats shown in the top number of the time signature.

## The Three Grade 1 Time Signatures

### 2/4 — Two Crotchet Beats per Bar

2/4 has 2 beats per bar, each a crotchet. It feels like a march: **STRONG–weak, STRONG–weak**.

```abc
X:1
T:2/4 — march feel
M:2/4
L:1/4
K:C
c d | e f | g2 |]
```

Common in marches, folk songs, and dance pieces.

### 3/4 — Three Crotchet Beats per Bar

3/4 has 3 beats per bar, each a crotchet. It feels like a waltz: **STRONG–weak–weak, STRONG–weak–weak**.

```abc
X:1
T:3/4 — waltz feel
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:C
c d e | f e d | c3 |]
```

Common in waltzes, minuets, and many classical pieces (e.g., Beethoven's Minuet in G).

### 4/4 — Four Crotchet Beats per Bar

4/4 has 4 beats per bar, each a crotchet. It feels like: **STRONG–weak–medium–weak, STRONG–weak–medium–weak**. Beat 1 is the strongest; beat 3 has a secondary accent. 4/4 is sometimes written as a large **C** (for "common time").

```abc
X:1
T:4/4 — common time
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
c e g e | f a c' a | g2 e2 | c4 |]
```

This is the most common time signature in Western music.

## Strong and Weak Beats

In any time signature, **beat 1 is always the strongest**. The beat pattern:

| Time sig | Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 | Beat 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2/4 | Strong | Weak | — | — |
| 3/4 | Strong | Weak | Weak | — |
| 4/4 | Strong | Weak | Medium | Weak |

> **Teaching tip:** Clap and count aloud together: "ONE-two, ONE-two" for 2/4, "ONE-two-three, ONE-two-three" for 3/4, "ONE-two-THREE-four" for 4/4. This is exactly what the AMEB aural test asks — clap the beat while the examiner plays a short piece.

## Counting Different Note Values in a Bar

The number of beats in a bar is fixed, but the notes inside can be any combination that adds up correctly.

**In 4/4:**
- 4 crotchets = 4 beats ✓
- 1 semibreve = 4 beats ✓
- 2 minims = 4 beats ✓
- 2 crotchets + 4 quavers = 2 + 2 = 4 beats ✓

**In 3/4:**
- A semibreve (4 beats) does **not** fit in a 3/4 bar — it is too long.
- 1 dotted minim = 3 beats ✓ (dotted notes are covered in Grade 2)

## How the examiner tests this

- "What does this time signature mean?" → State the number of beats and the type of beat.
- "What time signature is this piece in?" → The examiner plays a short piece and you identify 2/4, 3/4 or 4/4.
- "How many beats are missing from this bar?" → You must calculate the beats already present and state what is missing.
- "Is this in duple, triple or quadruple time?" → 2/4 = duple, 3/4 = triple, 4/4 = quadruple.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing the top and bottom numbers.** The top number = beats per bar. The bottom number = note type. These are not a fraction to be divided.
- **Saying 4/4 = "four-four"** without explaining its meaning. Always say: "four crotchet beats per bar."
- **Not adding up beats correctly.** Count every note and rest in the bar. A quaver = half a beat; two quavers = one crotchet beat.
- **Forgetting that 4/4 is sometimes written as C.** The "C" stands for "common time" and is identical to 4/4.

## Key points

- The top number = beats per bar; the bottom number = type of beat (4 = crotchet).
- **2/4**: 2 crotchet beats per bar — duple time — march feel.
- **3/4**: 3 crotchet beats per bar — triple time — waltz feel.
- **4/4**: 4 crotchet beats per bar — quadruple time — most common. Also written as **C**.
- Beat 1 is always the strongest in any time signature.
- Every bar must contain exactly the correct number of beats.
