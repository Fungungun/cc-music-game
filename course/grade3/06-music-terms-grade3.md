# Music Terms (Grade 3)

## What you will learn

- All 27 new terms introduced at Grade 3
- Pedal-related terms and notation terms (new categories at Grade 3)
- A complete review strategy for all three grades' terms

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 3 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to define all **Grade 1 and Grade 2 terms** plus the Grade 3 terms listed below. The exam may test *any* term from Grades 1–3. There are approximately **68 terms** in total across the three grades.

## Additional Tempo Terms

| Term | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Largo** | very slow and broad | — |
| **Larghetto** | rather slow (slightly faster than Largo) | Suffix *-etto* = "a little" |
| **Prestissimo** | as fast as possible | Superlative of Presto |

*Larghetto* and *Prestissimo* extend the tempo vocabulary to the extremes. The full spectrum:

**Largo → Larghetto → Lento → Adagio → Andante → Moderato → Allegretto → Allegro → Vivace → Presto → Prestissimo**

> **Teaching tip:** You now have 11 tempo terms — too many to memorise in a list. Instead, group them into three zones: slow (Largo, Larghetto, Lento, Adagio), medium (Andante, Moderato, Allegretto), fast (Allegro, Vivace, Presto, Prestissimo). Know the zones first, then fine-tune the order within each zone.

## Character and Expression Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Agitato** | agitated, restless |
| **Animato** | animated, lively |
| **Brillante** | brilliant, sparkling |
| **Con fuoco** | with fire |
| **Dolce** | sweet, softly |
| **Giocoso** | humorous, playful |
| **Largamente** | broadly |
| **Mesto** | sad, mournful |
| **Nobilmente** | nobly |
| **Semplice** | simply, plainly |
| **Teneramente** | tenderly |
| **Tranquillo** | tranquil, calm |

Many of these are contrasting pairs — learning them in opposites helps:

| Contrast pair | Term 1 | Term 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Fast / slow character | Agitato (restless) | Tranquillo (calm) |
| Joyful / sad | Giocoso (playful) | Mesto (sad) |
| Grand / simple | Nobilmente (noble) | Semplice (plain) |
| Fire / tenderness | Con fuoco (fire) | Teneramente (tender) |

## Pedal Terms

These terms are specific to piano performance (and stringed instruments with damper mechanisms):

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Una corda** | use the soft pedal (left pedal) — literally "one string" |
| **Tre corde** (or *tutte le corde*) | release the soft pedal — literally "three strings" |
| **Con sordino** | with mute / with soft pedal |
| **Senza sordino** | without mute / release soft pedal |

On a piano, the soft pedal (*una corda*) on a grand piano slightly shifts the hammers so they strike one string instead of three, producing a softer, slightly veiled tone. The term *tre corde* releases this effect.

## Dynamic Additions

| Term / Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ppp** | pianississimo — as soft as possible |
| **fff** | fortississimo — as loud as possible |
| **sfz** | sforzando — suddenly and strongly accented |

These extend the dynamic range beyond Grade 2's pp–ff spectrum.

## Notation and Performance Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Attacca** | go immediately to the next section without a pause |
| **Coda** | a concluding section at the end of the piece (marked with a coda sign 𝄌) |
| **Ossia** | an alternative (easier or ornamental) version of a passage |
| **Segue** | continue without pause (similar to Attacca) |
| **Subito** | suddenly (e.g., *subito p* = suddenly soft) |
| **Poco a poco** | little by little (e.g., *poco a poco cresc.* = getting louder gradually) |
| **Sempre** | always (e.g., *sempre f* = always loud) |

## Complete Term Review Strategy

With 68 terms across three grades, systematic review is essential. Suggested approach:

**Week 1:** All Grade 1 terms (17) — review by category (tempo, dynamic, articulation, signs)
**Week 2:** All Grade 2 terms (24) — review by category, then mixed quiz
**Week 3:** All Grade 3 terms (27) — review by category, then mixed quiz
**Week 4:** Random mixed review of all 68 — simulate exam conditions

The Terms Flashcards module uses **spaced repetition** (SRS) — it automatically prioritises terms you find harder and reduces frequency for well-known terms. Use it daily for 5–10 minutes in the weeks before the exam.

## How the examiner tests this

- "What does *agitato* mean?" → "Agitated, restless."
- "What is the difference between *una corda* and *tre corde*?" → "*Una corda* = use the soft pedal; *tre corde* = release the soft pedal."
- "List three Grade 3 terms that describe character." → Any three from the character list above.
- "What does *subito p* mean?" → "Suddenly soft."
- "What is *poco a poco* and give an example of how it might be used?" → "Little by little. Example: *poco a poco crescendo* = getting gradually louder, little by little."
- "Define *Prestissimo*." → "As fast as possible — faster than Presto."

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing *Largamente* with *Largo*.** *Largo* = very slow. *Largamente* = broadly (describes breadth of tone and phrasing, not necessarily extremely slow).
- **Saying *una corda* = "soft pedal" without explaining.** Add: "literally 'one string' — the left pedal on a piano."
- **Forgetting to include Grades 1 and 2 terms.** Grade 3 students are still expected to know all earlier terms — the exam draws from all three grades.
- **Mixing up *segue* and *attacca*.** Both mean "continue without pause" — in practice they are interchangeable. If asked, explain what they have in common.

## Key points

- Tempo extremes: **Largo** (very slow) · **Larghetto** (slightly less slow) · **Prestissimo** (fastest possible).
- Character contrasts: **Agitato** (restless) ↔ **Tranquillo** (calm) · **Giocoso** (playful) ↔ **Mesto** (sad).
- Pedal terms: **Una corda** = soft pedal on · **Tre corde** = soft pedal off.
- Notation: **Subito** (suddenly) · **Poco a poco** (little by little) · **Sempre** (always) · **Coda** (concluding section).
- Know all 68 terms across Grades 1–3 — the exam draws from all of them.
