# Extended Key Signatures and Scales (Grade 3)

## What you will learn

- Three new key signatures: E major (4#), B major (5#), Ab major (4b)
- The scales corresponding to these key signatures
- Completing the circle of fifths picture up to 5 sharps and 4 flats

## AMEB Syllabus Reference

> **AMEB Piano 2026 · Grade 3 · Section III**
> Candidates must be able to identify key signatures of up to **5 sharps** (B major) and **4 flats** (Ab major), name the major key and its relative minor, and write or name the notes of the **E major, B major and Ab major scales**.

## The New Key Signatures

### E Major — 4 Sharps: F# C# G# D#

Sharps in order: F# C# G# **D#** (the 4th sharp added is always D#).

```abc
X:1
T:E major key signature — 4 sharps
L:1/4
K:E
e4 |]
```

**Identify:** Last sharp = D#. One semitone above D# = E. ✓ Key = **E major**.
**Relative minor:** B minor (3 semitones below E: E → Eb → D → Db... wait — count down: E→Eb(1)→D(2)→Db(3)... Hmm. Actually: E → D# → D → C# = **C# minor**).

Let me recalculate: 3 semitones *below* E: E→Eb (1) → D (2) → C# (3) → **C# minor**.

**Relative minor of E major: C# minor.**

### B Major — 5 Sharps: F# C# G# D# A#

```abc
X:1
T:B major key signature — 5 sharps
L:1/4
K:B
b4 |]
```

**Identify:** Last sharp = A#. One semitone above A# = B. ✓ Key = **B major**.
**Relative minor:** G# minor (3 semitones below B: B→Bb→A→G# = **G# minor**).

### Ab Major — 4 Flats: Bb Eb Ab Db

Flats in order: Bb Eb Ab **Db** (the 4th flat added is always Db).

```abc
X:1
T:Ab major key signature — 4 flats
L:1/4
K:Ab
_a4 |]
```

**Identify:** Second-to-last flat = Ab. ✓ Key = **Ab major**.
**Relative minor:** F minor (3 semitones below Ab: Ab→G→Gb→F = **F minor**).

## The New Scales

### E Major Scale

Apply T T S T T T S from E:
E→F# (T), F#→G# (T), G#→A (S), A→B (T), B→C# (T), C#→D# (T), D#→E (S).

**Notes:** E F# G# A B C# D# E

```abc
X:1
T:E major scale
L:1/4
K:E
E ^F ^G A B ^c ^d e |]
```

### B Major Scale

Apply T T S T T T S from B:
B→C# (T), C#→D# (T), D#→E (S), E→F# (T), F#→G# (T), G#→A# (T), A#→B (S).

**Notes:** B C# D# E F# G# A# B

```abc
X:1
T:B major scale
L:1/4
K:B
B ^c ^d e ^f ^g ^a b |]
```

### Ab Major Scale

Apply T T S T T T S from Ab:
Ab→Bb (T), Bb→C (T), C→Db (S), Db→Eb (T), Eb→F (T), F→G (T), G→Ab (S).

**Notes:** Ab Bb C Db Eb F G Ab

```abc
X:1
T:Ab major scale
L:1/4
K:Ab
_A _B c _d _e f g _a |]
```

> **Teaching tip:** Ab major has an unusual property — it contains both Ab and Db, which students may incorrectly write as G# and C#. Use *flats* throughout for Ab major. The two spellings are enharmonically equal on the piano but different in notation.

## The Complete Circle of Fifths (Grades 1–3)

| Sharps | Key | Relative minor |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | C major | A minor |
| 1 | G major | E minor |
| 2 | D major | B minor |
| 3 | A major | F# minor |
| 4 | E major | C# minor |
| 5 | B major | G# minor |

| Flats | Key | Relative minor |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | C major | A minor |
| 1 | F major | D minor |
| 2 | Bb major | G minor |
| 3 | Eb major | C minor |
| 4 | Ab major | F minor |

## Grade 3 Harmonic Minor Scales

For Grade 3, the harmonic minor scales that become testable alongside the new keys:

- **B harmonic minor** (relative of D major): B C# D E F# G **A#** B (raised 7th = A#)
- **F# harmonic minor** (relative of A major): F# G# A B C# D **E#** F# (raised 7th = E#)
- **C# harmonic minor** (relative of E major): C# D# E F# G# A **B#** C# (raised 7th = B#)
- **G# harmonic minor** (relative of B major): G# A# B C# D# E **F##** G# (raised 7th = F##)

Note: B# = C and E# = F on the piano, but these spellings are theoretically correct.

## How the examiner tests this

- "Name the key with 4 sharps." → "E major."
- "What is the relative minor of B major?" → "G# minor."
- "Write the Ab major scale ascending on the treble clef."
- "How many flats does Ab major have? Name them." → "4 flats: Bb, Eb, Ab, Db."
- "What is the key signature of C# minor?" → "4 sharps (same as E major)."

## Common mistakes

- **Writing G# major instead of Ab major.** These are enharmonically equivalent on the piano, but the exam expects Ab major written with flat notation.
- **Forgetting D# in E major.** Students familiar with G and D major (2 sharps, 3 sharps) may forget that E major adds D# as the 4th sharp.
- **Miscounting relative minors for sharp keys.** C# minor (relative of E major) is easy to miscalculate — use the 3-semitones-below rule systematically: E → Eb → D → C# ✓.

## Key points

- **E major**: 4 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#). Relative minor: **C# minor**.
- **B major**: 5 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#, A#). Relative minor: **G# minor**.
- **Ab major**: 4 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab, Db). Relative minor: **F minor**.
- Sharps are added in order: F# C# G# D# A# — each new key is a 5th above the previous.
- Flats are added in order: Bb Eb Ab Db — each new key is a 5th below the previous.
